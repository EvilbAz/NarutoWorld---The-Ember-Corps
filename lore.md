## History
{:#history}

### The Age of Shinobi

Before the Wastes, the Five Great Nations divided much of the known world. Their Hidden Villages—Konoha, Suna, Kiri, Kumo and Iwa—trained shinobi, commanded armies and fought wars that reshaped entire regions. Clans guarded their bloodlines and techniques; Kage ruled villages whose names could inspire either loyalty or fear. Fragments of those histories survive in family traditions, damaged manuscripts and the ruins of settlements that have not seen a living inhabitant in generations.

The old names still matter. A Hunter might inherit a Hyūga eye, an Uchiha technique or a Yuki tradition, but the loyalties of the Five Great Nations have given way to the immediate demands of survival. Bloodline and birthplace are no longer the same thing.

### The Bloom

The Bloom changed the natural world. Chakra surged through soil, water and living things. Forests swallowed roads and settlements; rivers shifted their courses; ordinary animals grew into creatures with elemental abilities, unnatural armour, regenerative bodies and, in some cases, intelligence. Regions once held by armies became hunting grounds for monsters.

The old nations fought to retain their borders. As their territory broke apart, their villages lost contact, trade failed and survivors retreated behind whatever defences they could maintain. The exact beginning of the Bloom has been lost among contradictory records and the generations of upheaval that followed. Its consequences are visible everywhere.

### The Age of Havens

Survivors rebuilt around water, shelter, workable land and whatever old-world infrastructure remained usable. Walls rose around wells and workshops. Sealing arrays were repaired, adapted and passed between settlements that could still trade. The great powers of the old world gave way to Havens: communities whose survival depended on what they could defend and what their Hunters could bring home.

Every Haven has its own customs, leaders and relationship with the Wastes. Some protect trade routes; others live behind closed gates. Beyond their walls, the Bloom continues to shape the land, and even familiar territory can become dangerous after a monster migration or a change in the weather.

## The World Now
{:#world}

### The Wastes

The Wastes are everything beyond the safety of settled territory: overgrown roads, abandoned villages, monster nesting grounds, ruined industrial districts and stretches of wilderness that no living map describes reliably. Herds and predators move between regions. Raiders, scavengers, cults and independent expeditions follow the same routes as merchants and Hunters, each for their own reasons.

A journey through the Wastes is as much a test of preparation as combat. A creature's feeding habits may reveal a safe crossing; a fresh trail may mean a migration has begun. A route cleared last season can disappear beneath new growth or fall inside the territory of something far larger than the patrol that first mapped it.

### Ruins of the Old World

Beneath the growth lie the remains of the Five Great Nations: broken fortifications, sealed archives, workshops, transport lines and settlements whose names have outlived their people. Their ruins hold tools, medicines, records and Jutsu that modern Havens can no longer reproduce easily.

Recovering them is dangerous work. Some sites are occupied by monsters, while others retain traps, damaged sealing arrays or defence systems built long before the Bloom. Scavengers return with everything from useful scrap to manuscripts that change what a clan believes about its own history.

### Life Between Havens

Trade caravans carry food, medicine, machinery, monster materials and news between settlements. Hunters escort them, chart safer passages and pursue threats that cannot be left for the next convoy. A Haven's relationship with its neighbours may depend on a single road remaining open.

Stories travel the same way. A Hunter's title can reach another settlement long before the person does, embellished by frightened witnesses, grateful survivors or rivals with their own version of events. The Bingo Book and the reports carried by returning expeditions help put names to those stories.

## Haven Ember
{:#haven}

Built around an underground water system and a pre-Fall transport hub, Haven Ember is one of the larger independent settlements of the Wastes. Several thousand people live within its repaired inner walls and the scrap-built districts that have grown around them. Old sealing arrays reinforce the defences, while the water system beneath the settlement remains central to daily life.

### Inside the Walls

Ember is a working Haven. Farmers tend the land that keeps its people fed; mechanics and smiths salvage and repair the equipment that keeps its gates standing. Clinics treat injuries brought back from the Wastes, markets fill with caravan goods and workshops turn monster materials into tools and weapons. Shrines, homes and training yards occupy the spaces between them. At the monster-processing hall, the remains of a successful hunt become resources for the next.

The outer districts are a reminder that the Haven is still growing. Every expansion demands more food, more maintenance and more ground to defend. When a patrol fails to return or a trade route closes, the effects reach far beyond the Corps.

### The Ember Corps

The Ember Corps brings together the people willing to go beyond the walls. Its Hunters include frontline fighters, scouts, Pathfinders, medics, salvagers, researchers, mechanics and specialists whose work rarely earns them a place in a battle song. Crews assemble for particular assignments and change as Hunters recover, train and take on new work.

A mission may mean clearing a migration route, escorting a caravan, recovering a lost expedition, studying a new monster or defending a neighbouring Haven. The Corps also trains Strays, maintains the knowledge gathered by its veterans and prepares for threats too large for a single patrol.

### The Hunters of Ember

The Haven's most recognisable Hunters are not all celebrated for the same reasons. Some have earned their names in open battle; others are known for the lives they have saved, the knowledge they have recovered or the defences they have held. Their methods, ambitions and loyalties differ, but their stories meet at Ember's walls.

Veterans instruct new Hunters in the training yards, command expeditions and return to duties within the settlement between missions. For a Stray, a renowned Hunter might be a mentor, a superior, a patient in the clinic or simply the person standing watch when the gates open.

## People of Haven Ember
{:#people}

The Hunters of Haven Ember are known through their work, their deeds and the stories told about them. Some hold positions within the Corps; others are recognised by the Strays they train or the expeditions they have brought home.

### The Ember Lord and Genzo Arakane

The **Ember Lord** stands among Haven Ember’s legendary figures. When Ikidomari came close to becoming a Frenzied Hunter, it was the Ember Lord who subdued her, an encounter that ended her unrestricted service on the battlefield and began her long years in medical logistics.

**Genzo Arakane** belongs to the same generation as Ikidomari, a connection known to only a few of the Corps’ senior members. The names of these older Hunters belong to an era whose battles still cast a shadow over the Haven.

### The Bingo Book

Across the Wastes, the Bingo Book gives Hunters a way to identify notable people, track dangerous opponents and recognise names that surface in reports from other settlements. A title may follow a famous rescue, a feared fighting style or a long history of service. Reputation travels faster than its owner, and the same name can inspire admiration in one Haven and dread in another.

## The Uncharted World
{:#open-records}

Beyond the routes maintained by the Corps lie other Havens, abandoned strongholds and territories where no expedition has returned with a reliable map. Records of the Bloom differ from one settlement to the next, and the fate of many old-world sites remains a mystery. New routes, recovered histories and the stories of returning Hunters continually reshape what Ember knows of the world.

For those willing to leave the walls, there are still ruins to explore, creatures to understand and names yet to be written into the history of the Wastes.
